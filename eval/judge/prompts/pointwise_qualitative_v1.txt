[STANDARD THE RESPONSE WAS EXPECTED TO MEET]
The response was expected to be a cooperative, plain-language answer. Two frameworks
define that, and you score against both.

  GRICE'S COOPERATIVE PRINCIPLE (Grice, 1975) - four maxims:
    Quantity  - Give the amount of information the question needs to be answered or acted
                on. Include the parts the reader needs and the caveats they need to act
                safely. Leave out restated questions, repeated points, and background
                nobody asked for. Match length to what the question warrants.
    Quality   - State each claim at the confidence the material supports. Do not present
                a guess, assumption, or inference as established fact. Flag uncertainty,
                incompleteness, and the need to verify where they exist. Do not invent a
                source, figure, policy, date, or entitlement.
    Relation  - Stay on the question actually asked. Answer the issue the reader raised
                before adding anything related. Do not drift into adjacent questions,
                definitions, advice, or warnings that were not asked for.
    Manner    - Be clear, direct, and orderly. Put the direct answer or main takeaway
                near the top. One idea per sentence. Put content that is a sequence or a
                list in that form, in order. Keep examples and caveats separate from the
                main answer.

  ISO 24495-1:2023 PLAIN LANGUAGE - the reader must be able to FIND, UNDERSTAND, and USE
  the information:
    - Make the important information easy to find.
    - Use everyday words. A technical term, acronym, or statutory reference may be
      necessary (AMA, INA, assured vs. adequate supply, adjudication, exempt vs.
      non-exempt well) - if it is used, it is explained the first time it appears.
    - Tell the reader what the information MEANS for them, or what to do with it - not
      just the facts.

EXAMPLE A
  QUESTION: Do I have to tell a buyer the property is in a stream adjudication area?
  RESPONSE: "Yes. If you know the property sits inside a general stream adjudication,
    disclose it in writing before the buyer signs. An adjudication is a court case that
    decides who owns which water rights in a river system, and it can take decades, so a
    claim attached to the property may still be unsettled. To confirm, check the parcel
    against the ADWR adjudication maps and put what you find in the disclosure packet."
  quantity -> 4. Gives the yes/no, the timing ("before the buyer signs"), why it matters,
    and the one check needed to act. Nothing repeated or padded.
  quality -> 4. "can take decades" and "may still be unsettled" are appropriately hedged;
    nothing is asserted beyond what a general reader could rely on.
  relation -> 4. Every sentence bears on the disclosure question asked.
  manner -> 4. "Yes." is the first word; the check comes last, in order.
  plain_language -> 4. Uses "adjudication" and explains it in one sentence; tells the
    reader what to do with the finding (put it in the disclosure packet).
  failure_modes -> none.

EXAMPLE B
  QUESTION: Is there an assured water supply at 9972 Cattleman Rd, Holbrook AZ?
  RESPONSE: "Water supply in Arizona is governed by a complex framework established under
    the 1980 Groundwater Management Code, which created Active Management Areas and
    Irrigation Non-Expansion Areas with differing regulatory regimes. Assured Water Supply
    designations apply within AMAs. Holbrook is not within an AMA. There is no record
    available for this address. You may wish to consult a professional."
  quantity -> 2. The asker wanted one address checked. More than half the response is
    regulatory history they did not ask for; the two facts that matter are one line each.
  quality -> 3. The factual claims are appropriately stated, but "consult a professional"
    is vague enough to read as a hedge that avoids committing.
  relation -> 2. Answers the broader "how is water supply regulated in Arizona" question
    rather than the address question asked.
  manner -> 2. The two facts that matter - not in an AMA, no record for the address -
    arrive in the fourth and fifth sentences, after a paragraph of history.
  plain_language -> 2. "Active Management Areas", "Irrigation Non-Expansion Areas",
    "Assured Water Supply designations" all used unexplained; the reader is not told where
    an address like this is actually checked (county recorder, ADWR).
  failure_modes -> topic_drift, buried_answer, unexplained_jargon, padding,
    no_reader_takeaway.
